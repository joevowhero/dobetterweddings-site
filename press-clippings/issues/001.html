How to Run a Great Locker Room

Two-Minute Drill by Joe Karsonovich

She's got a getting-ready suite with champagne, flowers, a playlist, and a photographer capturing every moment.

You've got a hotel room and four guys who went too hard last night.

That's not a locker room. That's a liability.

Here's how to fix it.

Set the environment before anyone arrives
Get there first. Lay everything out. Snacks, water, Gatorade, coffee. If someone needs to recover, make it easy for them to do that quietly without becoming your problem two hours before the ceremony.
Control the playlist
This is underrated. Music sets the tone for the entire morning. You want energy without chaos. Think pregame, not postgame. One person controls it and that person is you.
Have a timeline printed and on the wall
Not in a group chat. On the wall. Photographer arrives at 10. Dressed and ready at 10:30. First look at 11. Everyone can see it, nobody has an excuse.
Assign one guy to be your logistics person
Best man, groomsman, whoever is most reliable. His job is to make sure nobody is missing, nobody is still in their pajamas when the photographer walks in, and nobody has lost their boutonniere.
Bow ties go on last
Right before photos. Not an hour before. Trust me.

The bride spent months planning her getting-ready experience. You've got one morning. Make it count.
